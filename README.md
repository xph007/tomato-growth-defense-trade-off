# Community assembly mechanisms of the rhizosphere microbiome mediating the host growth-defense trade-off
The rhizosphere microbiome functions as an extended phenotype for the host plant, yet whether its temporal succession is actively orchestrated to compensate for stage-specific physiological vulnerabilities remains elusive. Here, we combined longitudinal 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing, non-targeted metabolomics, and culturomics to decode the assembly rules of the tomato rhizosphere microbiome across four developmental stages. We revealed a defense-nutrition trade-off in community function: the seedling stage prioritizes the recruitment of a protective microbiome enriched in secondary metabolite biosynthesis pathways, whereas the fruit stage assembles a nutrient-mobilizing microbiome with enhanced capacity for the biogeochemical cycling of essential macronutrients. This succession is mechanistically driven by dynamic shifts in root exudation profiles, from sugars and short-chain acids in seedlings to specific long-chain organic acids in fruiting plants, which act as chemical cues for selective recruitment. In vivo validation using stage-specific SynCom confirmed the seedling-associated SynCom functioned as a biological shield, reducing bacterial wilt incidence from 80% to 15%, while the fruit-associated SynCom served as an external nutrient pump, increasing aboveground biomass by 272%. Our findings demonstrate that plants maximize fitness by actively outsourcing stage-dependent functions, specifically defense in juveniles versus nutrition in adults, to their microbiome, providing a blueprint for designing precision, stage-specific bio-inoculants for sustainable agriculture.
